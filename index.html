I build robots that help in emergencies.

In the Advanced Robotics and Controls Lab I've built a mobile manipulator and a shape-changing leg for search and rescue, working with my thesis advisor, Prof. Michael Yip.

I design the hardware: frames and mechanisms, motors and their control, and the tests that show whether a design does what the model says. I've been hooked on robots since a fourth-grade LEGO class.

What I'm working on

A leg that changes shape

A search and rescue robot has to cross rough ground quickly, then pull a heavy load once it reaches someone. This leg does both by changing its link lengths on the fly. The paper was accepted to ICRA 2026.

Background

It started in fourth grade with a LEGO Mindstorms robot and a lot of arithmetic about wheel rotations. My public school district had almost no STEM programs, so a Friday engineering class in middle school, with balsa gliders and spaghetti towers, mattered a lot.

At UCSD I joined Triton Robotics knowing less than most of my teammates and ended up leading the team. Along the way I worked on a solar water heater for an orphanage in Tijuana, and since 2024 I've been building rescue robots at ARCLab.
