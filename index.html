How I got here

  1. 4th grade

    LEGO and wheel rotations

    A few of us huddled around a LEGO Mindstorms robot after school, working out how many wheel rotations it would take to reach a marker across the room. Watching the math turn into a robot rolling across the floor is what got me hooked.

  2. Middle school

    Friday engineering class

    My public school district had almost no STEM programs, but there was one class called Engineering Design and Robotics. We flew balsa wood gliders across the gym, built towers out of spaghetti and marshmallows, and ran solar-powered robots on the basketball court.

  3. 2021

    Joining Triton Robotics

    Most of my teammates had run FRC, FTC, or VEX teams in high school. I didn't even know those existed. I started with less experience than anyone, worked up to leading the Hero robot's mechanical design, and ended up president of the 80-person team. What I loved most was helping newcomers go from intimidated to confident, because I'd been one.

  4. 2023

    Hot water in Tijuana

    With UCSD's Baja Solar group I worked on a solar water heater for an orphanage. Hot water there meant cooking, cleaning dishes, and more than a two-minute shower, and that made engineering feel very direct.

  5. 2024 to now

    Rescue robots at ARCLab

    I joined Prof. Michael Yip's lab to build hardware for emergency response. That became a mobile manipulator, a leg that changes shape, and a paper at ICRA 2026.

Where I'm headed. I came into grad school drawn to bio-inspired robots for search and rescue, and that's still my daily work. Increasingly I'm interested in medical and assistive robotics: haptics, soft robotics, and devices that touch a person, where mechanical design and careful testing decide whether it's safe.
